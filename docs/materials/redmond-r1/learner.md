# Draft learner activities — orient and locate

Use with the matching labeled or partial Redmond map, north arrow and scale. This packet is awaiting content review. A–D are map reference markers. Do not use it as a route or access guide.

1. Face directly from A toward C. Estimate the bearing clockwise from geographic north. Explain how you used the north arrow. Repeat with the paper turned a quarter turn: does the geographic bearing change?
2. Face directly from A toward B. Estimate the true bearing and explain why it is not exactly east. Use the straight line between markers, not the road's curves.
3. Locate the reference where Brown Street meets Cleveland Street. Mark it and name the two street clues you used.
4. Locate the reference where 161st Avenue Northeast meets Cleveland Street. Explain how this differs from the Brown Street junction.

For each answer, write your estimate/location and one sentence explaining your clues. The corresponding PDF worksheets provide answer space. On screen, draft questions can be inspected/printed but scored sessions are disabled pending review.

## Later-stage review activities

These are instructor-led drafting prompts, not approved navigation questions:

- Trace the drawn lines from A to B and then D. Distinguish a connection shown by the diagram from permission to travel along it. What information is missing?
- Sketch a possible return on the drawn lines. Can this packet establish that the reverse direction is permitted? Explain the uncertainty rather than guessing.
- Imagine the A–B portion of Cleveland Street is closed. Can this limited map establish a usable alternative? Mark where more map coverage and access information would be needed.
- Hide the labeled map, reconstruct A/B/C/D and the three named streets on blank paper, then compare with the original. Describe one feature you remembered and one you corrected.

Do not perform a walking/passenger observation from this packet until the instructor has separately reviewed the material and observation setting. No active-driving interaction is part of these activities.
